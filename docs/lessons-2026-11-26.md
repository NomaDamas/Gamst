# Lessons learned (2026-11-26, counting one person's words in real time)

Keep adding to this file. Each lesson says what happened, the evidence, and what we do now.

## Measurement

1. **One clip lies.** Settings chosen on the 뽀또 clip did not hold on the other three (attribution 8/45 across four clips
   with the same setup). Defaults are chosen on the owner's clip set, validated leave-one-clip-out.
2. **Score against the owner's ear, not against what looks plausible.** Without labels we kept reasoning about lips and
   voice scores; the owner's labels showed the real split at once: words heard 45/46, attributed right 8/45.
3. **Disclose leakage.** A 1.5 s voice sample cut from the test clip contained a counted word and made results look better
   (12/9). Words inside enrolment ranges are excluded from scoring and said so.
4. **Break errors down by type** (not heard / person given to someone else / someone else counted as the person /
   uncertain). The fix for each type is different; one total hides it.
5. **Measure before claiming.** Wrong statements this day: a FLEURS figure copied from a blog, a "realtime" claim for a 60 fps
   clip (28.5 s for 11.9 s), a count (10/8 that was 11/8). Every number now names its run.

## Attribution (who said the word)

6. **The voice reference matters more than the model.** A larger speaker model (w2v-BERT), separation (PixIT), lips (Light-ASD)
   and TSE (PS4) gave no gain; a reference from the same recording lifted the person's words from mostly below 0.5 to mostly
   above it (bottleneck doc) and misses fell from 37 to 1.
7. **An automatic reference can be wrong.** The "main speaker" of the first 15 s of 뽀또 mixed two people (self-similarity
   0.42) and made another man's words final as the person. A reference must pass a quality check; a sample recorded on the same
   input before counting is the safest.
8. **Deciding one short word at a time is weak.** A word is 0.3 s; a 1.5 s window holds both people. Long speaker segments
   (Precision-3) separate people far better; overlap remains a real limit.
9. **Live diarization on noisy broadcast audio mixed people** (Nemotron-3, Sortformer, Live-1); it does not decide attribution.

## Hearing (what was said)

10. **Recognisers invent words in long same-word context** (Scribe wrote 뽀또 where nothing was said); a second and third
    recogniser voting removed it (precision 1.00 on the vote runs).
11. **Recognisers write the same word differently** (뽀또 / 포토 / 포또; 씨발 / 씨팔 / 십팔 / 18): terms need spellings and
    whole-word matching; numbers need a flag.
12. **Server VAD in shop noise barely commits** (23 s to final); client commits give ~1-3 s.

## Realtime and HUD

13. **"Instant" must be visible at L1.** v5 counted at once on the HUD but L1 excluded the person's words provisionally, so the
    count rose only when verification corrected it 20+ s later. L1 counts every heard word; corrections come later, visibly.
14. **Catch-up must never drop audio.** Measured: 0 dropped, identical finals under forced overload.
15. **Verification regions follow speech** (0 utterances split vs 5 per run with fixed 15 s), but they shift diarization windows;
    anchor diarization context to each word.

## Operations

16. **Parallel heavy models crash a laptop.** Several agents loading models caused OOM and a full disk from swap. Every heavy job
    goes through `try-it/guard.sh` (one at a time, memory floor, kills only itself).
17. **/tmp is not storage.** The session scratchpad was wiped on restart; inputs live in `try-it/testbed/`.
18. **zsh does not split an unquoted variable**: `rm -rf $P` with several paths did nothing. Quote and loop.
19. **Never touch what is not ours** (other projects' temp dirs, other agents' files); clean only our own regenerable files.
20. **Hand off continuously.** Agents commit often and keep a handoff file; the public handoff and the session handoff are updated
    at every milestone, so a lost session or a new account can continue.
21. **Show, don't summarise, to the owner.** Labelling became fast only once the owner could play each candidate and press one
    key; review mode made GAMST vs truth visible.

## Added after v6

22. **Hosted recognisers vary run to run.** The same code gave 달 final 8/8 in one run and 12/12 in the next: single words flip
    between "heard by one recogniser" and confirmed. Report several runs, never one, before a default changes.
23. **Instant at L1 is a commit question, not a HUD question.** L1 now counts every heard word (32/32 heard person words), but the
    count still waits for the recogniser's turn commit (12 s soft cap): p50 1.8-6.9 s. Shorter turns (3 s) give 1.3-2.6 s but lost
    words. The trade-off is explicit and an owner decision.
24. **Combining a bad reference with a good one is still bad.** A mixed automatic reference averaged with a clean one let the other
    man score ~0.50 (threshold); replacing it fixed 뽀또 (6 right of 7). Check quality first, then replace or clean.

25. **Say which timing a video uses.** The first videos drew each count at the moment the word was said, from results computed
    after the whole clip (post-processing); that looks instant but is not what a live run shows. Videos now have two explicit
    modes: decided-time (what a live run shows) and `--at-spoken` (presentation replay of the final results, captioned as such).
26. **Honest is slow until the decision is fast; partials make it fast.** The first 뽀또 video drew each +1 at the word's capture
    time, after offline decoding, so it looked instant; the honest HUD (v6) draws a count when GAMST decided it, and L1 decided at
    the recogniser's turn commit: word -> HUD p50 1.7-7.8 s per clip, max 12.8 s. Counting a term the moment the live recogniser's
    partial hypothesis has held it for 2 revisions (state `heard`, voice checked on the sound so far, confirmed or withdrawn by the
    final of the same turn) gave p50 0.73 s, p95 2.2 s, max 3.4 s over 3 runs x 4 clips, with the same L1 and final accuracy;
    10 of 149 heard counts were withdrawn, visibly (2026-11-27, docs/handoff-feat-partial-counts.md). Speed came from deciding
    earlier, not from drawing earlier.

27. **A vote of weaker models removed a true word the best model heard.** The three-recogniser majority revoked 41.76 s in
    the 피파 clip (the person said it; only Scribe v3 Live, the best recogniser by published results, wrote it), and 28.10 s
    (뽀또), 27.1 s (몇기야) and 12.28 s x5 (달) in some runs. It had been added for one invented word (24.16 s). Counting
    heads treats a weaker recogniser's silence as evidence; it is not. The primary now stands unless contested, and a
    contested word is uncertain, not revoked (decision 31). And check a confidence signal before relying on it: Scribe's
    own word confidence was *lower* on the true words (41.76: 0.02) than on the invented one (24.16: 0.33).

28. **Recall and precision are a mode, not a single default.** Counting needs the best total; searching ("never miss the person")
    needs recall. One setting cannot serve both: on 뽀또, search mode found one more of the person's words (recall 78 % → 89 %) and
    counted five other people's words as candidates (precision 78 % → 53 %). Both modes exist (decision 33); results carry the mode
    and every candidate carries the reason it would have been left out.
29. **A live window must show the same states as the video.** The live window drew nothing for a sound-only run and only a raw count
    with a camera; the promo videos showed states the live product did not. The live panel now shows the same states and corrections;
    the remaining HUD gaps are listed in `docs/hud-demo.md`.

## Added 2026-11-27 (real-time pose)

30. **Measure where the time goes before changing the model.** The live slam run lost 190 frames because "pose needs 33 ms";
    the obvious fixes were a smaller model or smaller frames. Measured per step: MediaPipe's own inference was 33 ms on the
    CPU but 6-10 ms on its GPU delegate, which works on macOS once frames are 4-channel; then 8 ms per frame went into
    pushing 2.7 MB through the worker's pipe and 8 ms more into two image copies. Shared memory and one colour conversion
    took the round trip from 18 to 10.5 ms, with the same model and no lost accuracy. Decoding (1 ms) was never the problem.
31. **The live lane must say what it skipped, and the final count must come from every frame.** A lane that keeps only the
    newest frame skips evenly and declares it (0 lost), yet the same MediaPipe model counted 13-16 of 16 live, because it
    loses fast falls at lower frame rates (13/16 on a 30 fps copy, even with every frame). A verification lane that keeps
    every frame (video memory, then a spill file) and runs the more accurate RTMW behind settled it to 16/16 in all 9 runs
    (60 fps as-live and realtime, 30 fps), adding the missed ones visibly, 7-14 s later at 60 fps and 1-4 s at 30 fps.
    Speed at once and accuracy at the end are two lanes, as for words.
32. **"SOTA" is a published table, not this clip.** RTMW-l (COCO-WholeBody 70.1 AP) was equal to MediaPipe full by the rule
    and one slam worse by few-shot examples at 60 fps; it was better only at 30 fps. As verifier with its own few-shot
    examples it revoked one right live count. Which model verifies is a measured choice per movement, not a ranking.

## Added 2026-11-27 (owner discussion: one model, integrated design)

33. **"Done" means GitHub CI passed, not the local suite.** PR #373 was reported ready after the local suite (15 known
    errors), but GitHub's core job runs without numpy: the new shared frame memory imported numpy for every frame and 19
    new tests failed there. A numpy-free venv now reproduces CI locally (failures must equal Main's 32 by test name), and a
    PR is reported only after its CI is compared with Main's.
34. **Measure the cause before explaining it.** MediaPipe full's misses at 30 fps (13/16) were explained as its tracking and
    smoothing; a direct check showed VIDEO and IMAGE mode give identical keypoints, heights at 30 vs 60 fps differ by
    <= 0.07 shoulder widths, and a re-simulation of the rule on them counts 16/16. The misses come from GAMST's side
    (open). A model was blamed in a table for what may be our bug.
35. **A weak fast lane plus a strong verifier costs compute and can lower accuracy.** The verifier's own few-shot threshold
    revoked a right count (few-shot 14/16), and the fast lane skipped 101-483 of 714 frames under GPU sharing. The owner's
    rule since: one most accurate model counts every frame; realtime comes from making that model faster and from L2
    correction by the same model, never from skipping or a weaker model (decisions 37-41).
36. **Profile large models inside a memory budget.** A Sapiens2 1B fp32 profile took free memory to 1-6 % in seconds; the
    guard stopped only that job. Large models are tried in fp16 at batch 1 after checking free memory; L2 replicas are
    sized from free memory (decision 42).
37. **Answer the question the owner asked.** A shared link was meant as "what should GAMST adopt from this?" and was first
    answered as "what is this?". When the owner shares material, answer how it applies to GAMST, with a recommendation.

## Added 2026-11-27 (PR #373: one model, every frame, in real time)

38. **Disk is a resource too.** Free disk fell to 2.7 GB: every ONNX Runtime CoreML session (RTMW 220 MB plus 184 MB, YOLOX
    96 MB plus 31 MB) wrote a compiled copy into `$TMPDIR/onnxruntime-<UUID>-<pid>-*`, and a killed worker never removed
    it: 218 folders, 16 GB. Now every session compiles into one cache folder per model, compute unit and batch size
    (`ModelCacheDirectory`, `<root>/cache/coreml`, about 1.9 GB once for RTMW-l and YOLOX): measured, no new temp folder,
    and a second load takes 0.19 s instead of 4.6 s (`cache_test.py`). A worker also removes the temp copies of dead
    processes at start. The video spill file keeps 10 GB of the disk free (its bound is min(8 GB, free - 10 GB), checked at
    every write; the report, `get_pipeline` notes and the HUD say when it was bounded), and no heavy run starts below 10 GB
    free.

39. **A batch that changes size is not a batch on CoreML.** Eight crops per call cost 14-15 ms per crop instead of 23, but
    in the worker every new batch size cost about 300 ms more (CoreML specializes a dynamic shape again; even going back
    to a size seen before). One session per fixed size (8, 4, 2) never re-specializes; a call is split into those sizes.
    Evidence: `try_remote.py` (319-425 ms on each new size, then 43-153 ms), `testbed-2026-11-26.md` "One model, every frame".
40. **The first frames are the slowest; warm up before the camera starts.** The first live run with batching counted 16/16
    but its backlog reached 3.8 s, all at the start: each session's first call specializes it. Warming every session at load
    took the run's backlog to 0.4 s and its latency p50 from 1.8 s to 0.21 s (`b8_live_1` vs `b8_live_2`).
41. **A second replica on one GPU adds 19 %, not 100 %.** Two RTMW workers on the GPU gave 80.7 crops/s against 68.0 for
    one; the Neural Engine or the CPU beside the GPU each added about 15 crops/s without slowing it (`bench_replicas.py`).
    L2 is a way to stay live while behind, not free throughput; the start benchmark measures both workers together.
42. **Re-simulate inside the real reading, one factor at a time.** The earlier re-simulation of the slam rule (16/16 at
    30 fps) left out GAMST's visibility gate. Reading the same MediaPipe landmarks four ways (`mp_cause.py`) showed the
    person choice and the shoulder-width definition change nothing; the 0.5 visibility gate drops the wrist at the impact of
    5.03 s; 2.05 s is lost at 30 fps by every reading (lesson 34 answered).
43. **The frame reader must never wait for L2.** The first chunked post run hung: the reader waited for an L2 worker that
    was waiting for the reader's next frames. Found by a test with stand-in modules (`tests/test_realtime_pose.py`
    `PostChunks`); the reader now waits only for the live lane of a batching lane, never for its L2 chains.
44. **Test the straddling event, not only the totals.** The first L2 test passed by luck of where the cut fell; with three
    cut positions an event was counted twice. Cause: an output without its own times was read as having times
    (`[None, None]`), so the claims never dropped it. Events are now owned one by one by their end time, and a stored-record
    safety net drops the other lane's event within 100 ms (decision 53).
